# Legal research: charity campaign accounts in Vietnam

Issue: DOS/Give#11. Checked on 2026-10-31.

**This is desk research, not legal advice.** It reports what the texts say and lists
what to ask counsel. JOY decides the legal questions (P4 in
`docs/Give-Technical-Design.md` section 9) with counsel.

## 1. Summary

- **G1 verdict: partly confirmed.** Decree 93/2021/ND-CP does require a separate
  bank account for each campaign and forbids receiving after the committed receiving
  period ends, but only for campaigns about natural disasters, epidemics and
  incidents (Art. 9.1.a, 9.7, 17.2). For campaigns that support patients with
  serious illnesses the decree says neither thing (Art. 23). For any other charity
  purpose (education, environment, animals and so on) Decree 93 does not apply at
  all (Art. 1.1).
- For disaster, epidemic and incident campaigns the same decree adds rules the
  design does not have yet: a written notice to the commune People's Committee,
  fixed publication deadlines (1 to 3 days before receiving, then 15 and 30 days
  after), a surplus plan, and a duty to tell the bank to stop receiving. For
  organizations it also caps the receiving period at 90 days and distribution at 20
  days after receiving ends (Art. 8.2, 8.3); for individuals those two caps appear
  only in the notes of the decree's notice form, which is a question for counsel.
- Circular 41/2022/TT-BTC (accounting for social and charity activities) reaches
  wider than Decree 93: an individual who raises money for social or charity
  purposes keeps a ledger in fixed forms, uses a separate bank account (not the
  personal spending account), reconciles with the bank every month and publishes
  the reconciliation at the end of the campaign (Art. 10, 11). How far "social or
  charity" reaches (for example environment or animal campaigns) is not settled by
  the text.
- **Crypto (G2):** no text found that lets a campaign accept crypto. Since 2026 a
  crypto asset is a kind of digital asset and Civil Code property (Law 71/2025), but
  the pilot market reserves crypto-related services and advertising to providers
  licensed by the Ministry of Finance, and fines for unlicensed services have
  applied since 2026-09-01 (Decree 284/2026 Art. 7.4). Whether a crypto donation
  is a "payment means" under Decree 52/2024 or a gift of property is open.
- **Standard (non-transparent) bank account:** no official text found that requires
  a "transparent" bank product. The texts ask for a separate account (per campaign
  for disaster campaigns), plus, for individuals, bank reconciliation and its
  publication. Whether a standard account meets that is for counsel to confirm.

## 5. Verdict on G1

G1 in the design: "Decree 93/2021 requires an individual campaigner to open a
separate account for each campaign, and not to receive more money after the
deadline."

**Partly confirmed.**

- **Confirmed** for campaigns about natural disasters, epidemics and incidents:
  individuals (D93 Art. 17.2) and legal entities (Art. 9.7) open a separate
  account for each campaign, may not receive more after the committed receiving
  period ends, and must tell the bank that receiving has stopped. For
  organizations the decree also caps that period at 90 days (Art. 8.2); for
  individuals the 90-day cap appears only in note (4) of the Appendix form, and
  whether it binds them is question 4.
- **Not supported** for campaigns that support patients with serious illnesses:
  Art. 23 has no per-campaign account and no stop-receiving rule. TT41 Art. 10.4.a
  still asks individuals to keep charity money in a separate account.
- **Not applicable** to charity campaigns for other purposes: Decree 93 does not
  cover them (Art. 1.1). TT41 bookkeeping duties reach individuals' campaigns that
  help people in hardship (TT41 Art. 1.3); for other purposes, see question 1.

Context: a Ministry of Finance reply published on the Government portal on
2026-10-06 answered a reader whose question stated that no crypto provider had yet
been licensed; the reply restated NQ 05 Art. 7.2 and did not contradict that
premise ([baochinhphu.vn](https://baochinhphu.vn/giao-dich-tai-san-ma-hoa-khong-qua-to-chuc-duoc-cap-phep-co-the-bi-xu-ly-102261006081040169.htm)).
Decree 93 itself only deals with money (VND, with foreign currency converted) and
goods in kind; it does not mention crypto assets.

Reading for the design: the design's channel `crypto` (receiving addresses,
`chain_indexer`) would have Give show wallet addresses, index transfers and convert
them to VND.

- **Risk that applies now, to Give:** showing wallet addresses and indexing or
  converting transfers could be read as a crypto-related service or as advertising
  under NQ 05 Art. 4.4, fined under Decree 284 Art. 7.4.
- **Deferred risk, to donors:** a donor's transfer could be read as a trade outside
  a licensed provider under NQ 05 Art. 7.2 and 7.3; the Decree 284 Art. 9.1 fine
  starts six months after the first licence.
- **Unsettled:** Law 71 Art. 46 treats a crypto asset as Civil Code property, so a
  crypto donation may be a gift of property rather than a payment. Decree 52
  Art. 3.11 and 8.6 matter only if a crypto transfer counts as a payment means.
  Whether crypto can be gifted outside the pilot market is open.

G2 stays an open legal question; questions are in section 8.

## 7. Standard (non-transparent) account, for counsel

- No official text found that requires the bank product sold as a "transparent" or
  public-statement account. What the texts ask, by campaign class:
  - Disaster, epidemic and incident campaigns: a separate account per campaign, at
    a commercial bank for individuals (D93 Art. 17.2), at a commercial bank or the
    State Treasury for organizations (Art. 9.7, 9.1.a).
  - Patient campaigns and campaigns within TT41's scope: a dedicated account, not
    the personal spending account (TT41 Art. 10.4.a for individuals) or one not
    shared with other activities (TT41 Art. 8.2.a for organizations without
    separate charity accounting).
  - Individuals in all of these: monthly reconciliation with the bank, and the
    reconciliation or bank detail ledger published at the end (TT41 Art. 10.3).
  - That the account is in the campaigner's own name is an inference from D93
    Art. 17.2 and the ban on renting or lending accounts (Decree 52 Art. 8.5).
- A standard account that is opened only for the campaign and whose statement Give
  imports and publishes appears to fit that letter. The design's choice to prefer
  partner banks' transparent accounts (D4) is a product choice, not one the texts
  found here require. Bank formats are in issue DOS/Give#10 (bank statement research).

## 8. Open questions for counsel (input for P4)

1. **Scope.** Which regime governs `give` campaigns outside Decree 93 Art. 1.1
   (education, environment, animal welfare, community development)? Do
   environment and animal-welfare campaigns fall under TT41, whose Art. 1.3 speaks
   of helping people in hardship? Is there any account or deadline rule for them
   beyond TT41 Art. 10?
2. **Account reuse.** Does "a separate account for each campaign" (Art. 17.2, 9.7)
   forbid using the same bank account for a later campaign after the first one is
   closed and reconciled? The design allows release and rebind (section 2.2).
3. **Money after the deadline.** The texts forbid receiving after the committed
   period but say nothing about money that arrives anyway. Must the campaigner
   return it to the sender, may it go to the surplus plan (Art. 18.4), or something
   else?
4. **Caps for individuals.** Do Appendix notes (4) and (6) (90-day receiving
   period, distribution within 20 days) bind individual campaigners, given that
   Art. 8.2 and 8.3 sit in the section on organizations? Does the "commitment with
   contributors" exception in Art. 8.2 and 8.3 cover a campaign page that states a
   longer period?
5. **Patient campaigns.** Is a per-campaign account expected in practice for
   Art. 23 campaigns although the text does not say so? What evidence of a disease
   on the Ministry of Health list (Art. 3.3) is enough?
6. **Give's own role.** Is DOS, by hosting and promoting campaigns, itself among
   those who "take part in raising" contributions under Art. 2.1.g, with its own
   notice, account and publication duties? Does Give's VietQR display, statement
   import and matching stay outside the payment intermediary services listed in
   Decree 52 Art. 3.17, 3.18 and 22.1 (collection support, payment gateway)?
7. **Minimum contribution.** Does Art. 4.3 forbid a platform-set minimum donation
   amount, or only compulsory levies?
8. **Donor data.** TT41 form S01CN/XH-TT asks for donor information such as name
   and address. How does that fit guest and anonymous donations, and personal data
   law?
9. **Local government reform.** Decree 93 refers to district-level People's
   Committees (Art. 2.1.c, 18.1). Local government has run on two levels since
   2025-07-01 (press pointer:
   [baochinhphu.vn](https://baochinhphu.vn/toan-van-28-nghi-dinh-ve-phan-quyen-phan-cap-phan-dinh-tham-quyen-giua-chinh-phu-va-chinh-quyen-dia-phuong-2-cap-102250613104134297.htm)).
   Which authority now receives the notices and guides distribution?
10. **Sanctions.** What are the administrative and criminal consequences for an
    organizer who breaks Decree 93 (for example receiving after the deadline), and
    can any liability reach the platform?
11. **Crypto.** Under Law 71/2025, NQ 05/2025, Decree 284/2026 and Decree
    52/2024: may a campaign accept crypto at all, as a gift of property or
    otherwise; would Give showing wallet addresses or indexing transfers be an
    unlicensed crypto-related service or advertising; would a donor's transfer be a
    trade outside a licensed provider once NQ 05 Art. 7.2 applies; and could a
    licensed provider ever act as the receiving channel?
12. **Transparent account.** Is a standard dedicated account enough under the
    texts in section 7, or do banks, the State Bank or local authorities expect the
    transparent product?

## 9. Proposed design changes (not implemented)

These are proposals for the design owner and the Money PO. Nothing here changes
`docs/Give-Technical-Design.md` or any code.

1. **Legal class per campaign.** Add `campaigns.legal_basis` with values such as
   `d93_disaster` (disaster, epidemic, incident), `d93_patient` and
   `other_charity`, set by the organizer and checked in review. The rules below key
   off it. Category stays a discovery aid (section 3).
2. **Receiving period.** For `d93_disaster`, require `ends_at`. For organizations,
   enforce `ends_at - starts_at <= 90 days` at review and show the distribution end
   date (`ends_at` + up to 20 days) on the campaign page (D93 Art. 8.2, 8.3). Apply
   the same to individuals only if counsel answers question 4 that the form notes
   bind them.
3. **No reuse of a receiving account for a new `d93_disaster` campaign** until
   counsel answers question 2: `account_bindings` release stays as designed, but a
   released account cannot be bound to a new campaign of that class (D93 Art. 17.2,
   9.7).
4. **Stop receiving at `ends_at`** for `d93_disaster`. Hide QR and account details
   the moment the campaign closes, generate a "stop receiving" letter for the
   organizer to send to the bank, and record when it was sent. Keep the
   `after_close` flag for money that still arrives, and add a resolution step per
   counsel's answer to question 3 (D93 Art. 17.2, 9.7).
5. **Commune notice** for `d93_disaster`. Generate the Appendix notice form from
   campaign data (items 1 to 10) plus the organizer's identity block, which needs
   new organizer fields, and record the date it was sent to the commune and any
   proof. Gate `approved` on it (D93 Art. 17.1, 6.5).
6. **Disclosure deadlines** for `d93_disaster`. Track and show the receiving
   details 1 to 3 days before receiving starts, totals received within 15 days
   after receiving ends, and totals distributed within 30 days after distribution
   ends (D93 Art. 14.4, 19.3). Tie the binding release rule to these dates instead
   of a flat "closed 30 days".
7. **Surplus step** for `d93_disaster`. At close, the organizer records a surplus
   plan (agreed with donors, or transfer to the Fatherland Front), paid out as an
   explained disbursement (D93 Art. 18.4, 10.6.c).
8. **Costs declared up front.** A campaign states whether costs will be paid from
   donations; such payouts use a `campaign_cost` category, totalled on the public
   page (D93 Art. 19.1, 24.2, 12.3).
9. **TT41 outputs** for individual campaigns in TT41's scope. Export S01CN/XH-TT
   from matched bank transactions (credit date from the statement), S03CN/XH-TT
   from disbursements (add recipient address; keep the signed confirmation as a
   `receipt` evidence file), and B08CN/XH-TT at close; store a monthly bank
   reconciliation record and publish the final one (TT41 Art. 10, 11).
10. **Patient evidence.** For `d93_patient`, require evidence that the disease is
    on the Ministry of Health list and record the patient's commune for the
    organization's notice (D93 Art. 3.3, 23.1).
11. **Minimum contribution.** Make `min_contribution` for `give` campaigns a
    technical floor only, pending question 7 (D93 Art. 4.3).
12. **Crypto channel stays off** for VN campaigns until counsel answers question 11.
    Keep `channel='crypto'` out of M1 to M4 as the roadmap already does.

## 10. Gaps and limits

  (no amending or replacing decree found) and on the decree's own text. This is
  not a certified status check.

  repeals 54 Ministry of Finance circulars. Its official file
  ([vanban.chinhphu.vn docid 220131](https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?pageid=27160&docid=220131&classid=1))
